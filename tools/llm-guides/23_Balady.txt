Balady — municipal licences, building permits, and complaints
Official platform: Balady https://balady.gov.sa
Mobile app: Balady
Ministry: Municipalities and Housing

What Balady is
Balady is the national municipal platform. People and businesses use it for a commercial activity licence (the shop or office licence), a building permit, a fencing permit, a demolition permit, municipal complaints, and related housing and municipal requests. A business that opens a shop, restaurant, clinic, or office usually cannot rely on a commercial registration alone. The activity also needs the municipal licence that matches the location.

Commercial licence, in practice
1. Create or sign in to a Balady account. Individuals often use National Single Sign-On.
2. Start a commercial activity licence (issuing a municipal licence).
3. Select the activity from the activity list. The activity must match what the shop actually does. A restaurant activity is not a grocery activity.
4. Link the shop location. The unit should have a national address and, where required, an Ejar contract or an ownership deed.
5. Complete the requirements Balady lists for that activity. Food activities usually need extra clearances such as civil defence and, where the activity requires it, a health certificate. Balady shows the exact list on the request. Do not assume every shop needs the same papers.
6. Pay the licence fee shown on Balady. Fees depend on the activity, the size, and the city. Never invent the fee. Read the invoice.
7. Download the licence from Balady when the request is approved. Renew it before it expires from the same account.

Building permit
A building permit is also a Balady request. The owner or the engineering office submits the land, the drawings, and the supervision office Balady asks for. Work should not start before the permit is issued. A fencing permit and a demolition permit are separate requests. Inspections during construction are part of the permit, not an optional visit.

Complaints
Street cleaning, waste, encroachment, and similar municipal issues can be reported in the Balady app. The report should include the location. Balady gives a request number.

Frequently asked questions
Q: I have a commercial registration. Can I open the shop?
A: The commercial registration is Ministry of Commerce. The right to operate in that unit is the Balady municipal licence, plus any extra clearance Balady lists for the activity.
Q: How much is a Balady licence?
A: The fee is calculated inside the Balady request. It is not one national price.
Q: My restaurant licence was rejected.
A: Open the request notes. The usual causes are the wrong activity, a missing civil-defence or health paper, or a location that does not match the deed or the Ejar contract.
Q: Hello, I want a balcony permit.
A: Greet them, then point them to a building or fencing request on balady.gov.sa and tell them not to build before the permit is issued.
